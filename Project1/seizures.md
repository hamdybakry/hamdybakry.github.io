# Case scenario

## History

- A pregnant woman is brought to the emergency department having suffered a seizure in the park 20 min ago. She had been alone at the time but the seizure was witnessed by another woman who said that she had stood up from a bench and then suddenly dropped to the ground. She thought she may have hit her head on the side of the bench with the fall.
- Her arms and legs had been shaking and then were ‘stiff and trembling’ for about 40 s. The woman’s face had gone dusky and there was some frothing at the mouth. She noticed that the woman’s trousers were wet afterwards.
- When the fit stopped the woman had appeared unconscious for a few minutes and then showed some response to being talked to but seemed confused and drowsy.

## Examination

- She appears to be in her 30s and in the third trimester of pregnancy. She is now conscious but still drowsy and her Glasgow Coma Scale is 9/15. Her blood pressure is 140/98 mmHg and heart rate 104/min. Examination shows no obvious cardiac or chest abnormality, and on abdominal palpation there is no apparent tenderness. The uterus feels approximately 30-week size (midway between umbilicus and xiphisternum), and a fetus can be palpated, cephalic with 4/5 palpable. Reflexes are brisk and plantar reflexes are upgoing.
- No investigation results are yet available.

## Questions

**What is your provisional diagnosis and how would you manage this woman in the first instance?**

## Prevent a further seizure OR Make a certain diagnosis?

- Any woman with a seizure in the second half of pregnancy should be assumed to have eclampsia until proven otherwise.
- ➤ OBSTETRIC EMERGENCY …
	- Keep airway patent
	- 2 g MgSO4 should be given as an IV bolus, followed by an infusion of 2 g/h.
- ➤ Then …
	- Urine sample for proteinuria.
	- Fluid restricted initially to 80 mL/h.
	- Blood tests: full blood count, urea and electrolytes, uric acid, liver function tests, coagulation screen, and group and save.
	- Transfer to HDU with continuous ECG and CTG monitoring.

---

- The woman’s husband arrives shortly and explains that she is a known epileptic who has grand mal seizures every few days, despite drug treatment.
- **How should your management alter now?**

---

The fact that the woman has epilepsy strongly suggests that this fit is caused by the epilepsy.

- The initial management was still correct
- You will not be sure that the fit was due to epilepsy until the urinalysis has been confirmed to be normal and the blood pressure, initially high, has normalized, the reflexes returned to normal and the blood tests results are found to be normal.
- Reflexes are commonly brisk, with upgoing plantar responses in the post-ictal phase.

---

- This woman regained full consciousness after half an hour and the blood pressure was normal with negative urinalysis and normal blood results. The magnesium was thus discontinued and she was planned for neurological review within the next few days to discuss compliance and drug regime.

# Overview: Epilepsy in Pregnancy

## **Prevalence**: 0.6% of pregnant women have epilepsy.

- Seizure disorders:
	- **Partial seizures** (do not involve both hemispheres)
		- **Simple**: no loss of consciousness
		- **Complex**: consciousness may be impaired
	- **Generalized seizures** (involve both hemispheres)
		- **Absence**: duration <20 s (“petit mal”)
		- **Tonic-clonic**: duration up to several minutes (“grand mal”)

## Maternal risks

- **Seizure Frequency**: Can increase (37%), decrease (13%), or remain unchanged (50%).
- **Risk Factors for ↑ seizure frequency**: 
	- Poor seizure control before pregnancy
	- Discontinuing medication
	- Hyperemesis
	- ↑ plasma volume → ↓ drug levels
- **Fetal Impact**: Generally tolerates seizures but increased risk with status epilepticus.

## Fetal risks

- **Congenital Anomalies**: 2–5% risk, primarily due to anticonvulsant medication. 
	- Women not on medication also have a higher risk than the general population.
- **Risk Factors**:
	- **Multiple drugs** increase risk more than monotherapy.
	- **High doses** increase risk; dividing doses may help.
- **Recommendations**: Folic acid (5 mg daily) reduces risk of NTDs.

## Neonatal Risks from Anticonvulsants

- Fetal anticonvulsant syndrome (dysmorphic facial features, major and minor structural anomalies, learning difficulties, and/or behavioral problems)
- Neonatal withdrawal.
- Vitamin K deficiency (enzyme inducers) and hemorrhagic disease of newborn.
- Developmental delay and behavioral problems.
- Epilepsy in the offspring of an epileptic mother.

*Valproate Use

- Avoid valproate in women of childbearing age unless necessary.
- Contraception advice and specialist supervision required.

## DDx of First Seizure in Pregnancy

- Eclampsia
- Epilepsy
- Infections: encephalitis, abscess
- Metabolic causes:
	- Drug/alcohol withdrawal
	- Drug toxicity
	- ↓ Glc, ↓ Na, ↓ Ca
- Vascular causes:
	- Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis
	- Cerebral infarction or hemorrhage
	- TTP
- Space-occupying lesion

**All patients who present with their 1st seizure in pregnancy should:** 

- have brain imaging CT/MRI 
- be reviewed by a neurologist

# Management of Epilepsy in Pregnancy

## Pre-Pregnancy Counseling

- Involve neurologist for clarity on diagnosis.
- Optimize treatment with the least number of drugs at the lowest dose.
- In conjunction with epilepsy team, consider stopping drugs if seizure-free for over 2 years.
- Folic acid (5 mg) recommended at least 12 weeks before conception till delivery.
- Risk of epilepsy in the child: 4% if one parent affected, 15% if both parents affected

## Antenatal Care

- Do not change medication if well-controlled and ensure compliance.
- **Detailed anomaly scan** and possible **fetal echocardiography**.
- Monitor antiepileptic drug levels if needed; dose may need adjusting.
- Insufficient evidence for routine 3rd trimester maternal vitamin K.
- General Advice: showers rather than baths, avoid sleep deprivation

## Intrapartum Care

- Aim for vaginal delivery; seizures during labor are not an indication for cesarean unless in status epilepticus.
- Maintain usual anticonvulsants during labor.
- Control seizures with benzodiazepines.

## Postnatal Care

- Neonatal vitamin K to ↓ risk of hemorrhagic disease of the newborn.
- Breastfeeding is encouraged.
- Slowly reduce increased pregnancy AED doses postpartum.
- Ideal contraception: intrauterine device.
- General advice ...
- Awareness regarding risk from sudden unexplained death in epilepsy (SUDEP)
